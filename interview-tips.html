Master Your Interviews

Interviewing is a skill—and like any skill, it gets better with practice. Whether you're preparing for your first internship or your dream job, our interview resources will help you present your best self to potential employers.

Tell Me About Yourself (TMAY)

"Tell me about yourself" is one of the most frequently asked questions in interviews—and often the first question you'll hear. Your response should highlight your education, technical achievements, and business-related accomplishments while demonstrating why you're the perfect fit for the role.

Think of your answer as a longer version of an elevator pitch and a preview to the rest of the interview. This is your chance to set the tone and remind the interviewer who you are and why they should be interested in you.

Key Components of TMAY

1. Who Are You?

Interviewers talk to many people, so remind them who you are and make yourself memorable.

  • Restate your name — This helps interviewers connect the face to the resume
  • Include something descriptive about who you are — Mention your program, year, or key characteristic (e.g., "I'm a first-year Master's student in Information Analysis")
  • Pro Tip: If you have multiple names you go by, let the interviewer know which you prefer

2. Past and Current Work

The hiring manager likely invited you to interview because something on your resume caught their attention. This is your chance to expand on it.

  • Review themes and generalities — What patterns do you see in your experience?
  • You can discuss items from your resume — It's perfectly fine to revisit experience you've already submitted. Provide brief examples (save the detailed stories for follow-up questions)
  • Connect experience to the role — Show how your background has prepared you for this specific opportunity

3. Strong Wrap-Up

End with clarity and leave a memorable impression about your future and fit for the role.

  • Look to the future: Help the interviewer envision you in the role. How do your goals align with theirs? What will you contribute? How will you grow?
  • Three Key Takeaways: Summarize three things you want the interviewer to remember about you throughout your conversation together

Pro Tips for Success

📍 Focus on What Interests the Interviewer

Demonstrate the qualities they want. Your areas of expertise should align with the company's needs and the job description.

  • Match your skills to required qualifications
  • Highlight relevant project experience
  • Show genuine excitement for the position

⏱️ Keep It to 2 Minutes

Your answer should be concise and impactful. Practice timing yourself to stay within the 2-minute window.

🎯 Tell a Comprehensive Story

Don't just list your resume. Weave your experiences into a narrative that shows growth and intentionality.

🔗 Connect to the Role

Every detail you share should connect back to why you're interested in and prepared for this specific opportunity.

Strategy 1: Themes—Interest and Preparation

Use this framework to structure your answer around themes that highlight your fit for the role:

Opening:

"My name is [Your Name], and I am a [descriptive title, e.g., 'first-year Master's student in Information Analysis']."

Professional Focus:

"I have expertise and interest in [professional areas related to the position]."

Relevant Experience:

"I have had internships in [relevant field] / I have project work in [relevant skill], which has prepared me to [job expectation]. While my resume covers my full background, I'm most excited about [specific accomplishment or skill]."

Key Strengths:

"Three things you should know about me are: [1] [strength], [2] [strength], and [3] [strength]. These are the priorities I'd like to highlight as we talk today."

Why this works: This approach helps the interviewer understand your priorities and sets the tone for the conversation ahead.

Strategy 2: Themes—Timeline

Use this framework to walk the interviewer through your journey and trajectory:

Your Background:

"My name is [Your Name]. My background is in [education and experiences that prepared you for this field]."

Current Focus:

"My current work and efforts focus on [what you're doing now and how it relates to this role]."

Future Vision:

"My future goals are [career aspirations that align with the position]. I'm excited about this opportunity because [how it fits into your path]."

Why this works: This narrative approach shows growth, intentionality, and how the role fits into your bigger picture. Be honest, but always relate your goals to the potential job responsibilities.

Practice Makes Perfect

The best way to master your "Tell Me About Yourself" answer is through practice. Here's how to get started:

  • Write it down: Draft your answer using one of the strategies above
  • Say it out loud: Practice speaking your answer to internalize it
  • Time yourself: Aim for 90 seconds to 2 minutes
  • Get feedback: Practice with a friend, mentor, or career coach
  • Tailor for each interview: Adjust your answer slightly for each company or role
  • Keep practicing: The more you interview, the more natural this answer becomes
